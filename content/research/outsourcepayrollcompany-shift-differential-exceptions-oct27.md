Published October 27, 2026.

This review groups shift differential exceptions by the condition that produced them. It helps a payroll team decide whether to fix a timekeeping code, a schedule practice, or a written rule.

## Research question and decision use

The question is which conditions generate shift differential exceptions and whether they cluster by shift code, supervisor, or rule version. The result can guide configuration and training. It should not be used to rank supervisors or employees.

## Population and eligibility

Include every timekeeping record in the pay period that carries a shift code or crosses a premium boundary. Record the export time, the pay period, the systems, and the exclusion rule for employees on leave or without punches. Freeze the export before review.

## Exception definitions

Define the categories before looking at the data: missing code, code conflict, partial shift, boundary crossing, rate mismatch, and rule-version mismatch. A record can have more than one category; require the reviewer to choose a primary category and list secondary ones.

## Evidence and rule versions

Attach the punches, the schedule, the applicable rule version, and the payroll line to each exception. Record the rule effective date and the source document. When two rule versions could apply, keep both and flag the conflict instead of selecting the one that matches the payroll result.

## Analysis plan

Report exception counts by primary category, shift, and rule version, with the number of records checked as the denominator. Show how many exceptions led to a correction and how many were resolved as no change. Inspect a sample of narratives before concluding that a code or a person is the cause.

## Sensitivity and exception review

Repeat the counts after excluding partial shifts and after combining all boundary-crossing cases. If the ranking of categories changes, report both views. Describe material cases that do not fit the categories rather than forcing them into an average.

## Privacy and limitations

Aggregate the report and avoid employee-level detail outside the payroll review. The review observes timekeeping and payroll records and cannot determine whether a labor agreement was interpreted correctly. Schedule changes, daylight-saving transitions, and system rounding may affect the pattern.

## Replication package

Retain the timekeeping export, rule versions, exception codebook, reviewer assignments, payroll register extract, and calculations. A later review should use the same categories or document the change before comparing periods.
